Loan programs

Options, described without a quote.

These are the paths Colorado buyers and homeowners usually ask about. Details stay general here on purpose. Eligibility, documentation, and any loan terms are confirmed for your file. Nothing below is an offer or a commitment to lend.

Conventional

A common mortgage for a home you will live in, and in some cases a second home or an investment property. Credit, cash to close, and the property all have to satisfy underwriting.

FHA

Insured by the Federal Housing Administration. Buyers often ask about it when they want guidelines that can be more flexible. Mortgage insurance and property standards apply. Approval is not automatic.

VA

For eligible veterans, service members, and certain surviving spouses. What is available depends on VA entitlement and the lender’s review. Scott is a veteran and can help you see whether an eligibility check is worthwhile. Read the Colorado VA guide.

USDA

Limited to eligible properties in qualifying rural areas, including some outlying parts of Colorado, and subject to income limits. Location is the first question, not a promise that a home qualifies.

Down payment assistance

State and local programs may help with cash a purchase requires. Income, price, education, and funding all matter, and programs open and close. Start an assistance check or read the Colorado guide.

Refinance

A refinance replaces the mortgage you already have. People ask about it when their plans for the home have changed. Whether a new loan fits depends on your current loan, your credit, and the property. This page does not estimate a payment or a savings figure.

Selling and buying the next house

If you already own and want a different home, the useful question is how the sale and the next financing fit on one timeline. Visit the move-up page. Using that page does not require you to hire Scott as your broker.

It is a review of the information you provide at that time. It can help you shop with a clearer picture. It is conditional, it can change or be withdrawn, and it is not an approval, a commitment to lend, or a promise of any specific loan terms.
