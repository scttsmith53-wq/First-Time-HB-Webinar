VA Loans in Colorado (2026)

Zero down, no monthly mortgage insurance, and a benefit you can reuse — explained plainly by a veteran. Last reviewed August 24, 2026.

How much down payment do you need for a VA loan?

For most eligible veterans, zero. VA loans allow 100% financing with no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance.

You still cover certain closing costs, but those can often be offset with seller concessions negotiated into the contract — so many veterans buy with very little out of pocket. Subject to eligibility and approval.

Can you use a VA loan more than once?

Yes — it's reusable. Once you sell and pay off a VA loan, your entitlement is typically restored, and in some cases you can hold two VA loans at once.

The "you already used it" belief keeps a lot of veterans from a benefit they still have. It's worth checking your actual entitlement rather than assuming.

What is the VA funding fee, and who's exempt?

A one-time fee that keeps the program running; it varies by down payment and first-vs-subsequent use, and it can be rolled into the loan.

Veterans receiving VA disability compensation, and certain others, are exempt from the funding fee entirely. Your exact fee or exemption is confirmed during the loan process.

Does a VA loan have mortgage insurance?

No — no monthly PMI, ever. That alone often makes a VA payment lower than a comparable conventional loan at the same price.

Can the seller help pay a veteran's closing costs?

Yes. Seller concessions can be negotiated in the contract to cover closing costs and certain fees.

Concessions are negotiated deal by deal, so they aren't guaranteed — but for many veterans they're the difference between "someday" and buying now.

I was told I can't use my VA benefit — is that true?

Usually, no. Veterans are often wrongly told their credit, a past use, or a prior home disqualifies them.

The only way to know is to have your eligibility and entitlement checked directly. It's free to find out — and it's the benefit you earned.
