Down Payment Assistance in Colorado (2026)

What it is, who qualifies, and how it makes buying possible sooner than most renters think. Last reviewed August 24, 2026.

What is down payment assistance (DPA)?

Help — usually a grant or a second mortgage — that covers some or all of your down payment, and sometimes closing costs. In Colorado it comes mainly through CHFA (the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority) and, in the Denver metro, metroDPA.

It pairs with a first mortgage (FHA, VA, USDA, or conventional). It's the piece most renters have never heard of — and it's often what closes the gap between renting and owning. Subject to eligibility and availability.

Who qualifies for down payment assistance in Colorado?

It varies by program, but common requirements are: under an income limit, under a purchase-price limit, a minimum credit score, a homebuyer education course, and using the home as your primary residence.

You don't always have to be a first-time buyer. Limits change over time and by county, so eligibility has to be confirmed for your specific numbers.

Do you have to pay it back?

Depends on the structure. Some assistance is a grant you never repay; some is a second mortgage repaid when you sell or refinance, or gradually.

Which one applies depends on the exact program — worth understanding up front so there are no surprises later.

Can you use DPA with an FHA or VA loan?

Yes — DPA is built to pair with a first mortgage (FHA, conventional, USDA, and in some cases VA).

Since VA already allows zero down, assistance is most often paired with FHA or conventional loans to cut the cash you bring to closing.

How much can you get?

It varies — usually a percentage of the loan or a dollar cap, not one fixed number. In practice it can cover a meaningful share of what you need.

Exact figures depend on the program and your loan and change over time, which is why a quick eligibility check beats guessing.
